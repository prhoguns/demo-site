    Ministries/Seniors

    Seniors

    The Prime Time 55+ club, Communion at home, anointing Masses, rides to church and a phone that rings — the parish looks after the people who built it.

    A third of our parishioners are over 65, and many of them have been here since the church was built. This page gathers everything the parish offers older members — for fun, for prayer, and for the harder seasons of illness and loss.

    Wednesdays after the 8:30 Mass

    Prime Time 55+

    Coffee and pastries in the hall at 9:15, then something different each week: a speaker (a pharmacist on medications, a lawyer on wills, a police officer on scams), cards and games, chair exercise, a film, or a bus trip — Niagara's shrines in May, the Martyrs' Shrine in June, a Christmas lunch in December. About sixty members; $10 a year. New members are welcomed with a name tag and a seat at a table of people who will remember your name.

    Contact: Anna Ferraro · primetime@stcolumbatoronto.ca

    Communion at home

    Weekly or as wished · Lay Pastoral Visitors

    If you cannot come to Mass, a screened visitor brings Holy Communion, the Sunday readings and the bulletin, and stays for a visit. Call the office to arrange.

    Anointing & visits

    Anointing Masses

    February 11 (Our Lady of Lourdes) and October · 11:00 AM, lunch after

    A Mass at which anyone who is elderly or unwell may receive the Anointing of the Sick, followed by lunch in the hall.

    Rides to Mass

    Sunday 9:00 and 11:00 AM · volunteer drivers

    Door-to-door rides within the parish for those who no longer drive. Book by Thursday with the office. Wheelchair-accessible taxi vouchers are available for those who need them.

    Phone tree & friendly calls

    Weekly

    A volunteer phones each week to say hello, check in and pass on news. Started in 2020 and never stopped.

    Long-term care visits

    Weekly · two nearby homes

    Communion services and visits at the two long-term-care homes in the parish; monthly Mass at each with Fr. Nguyen.

    Bereavement support

    Groups and companions

    For the loss of a spouse, a friend, a sibling.

    Bereavement ministry

    Practical support

    • Accessibility: step-free entrance on the east side, elevator to the hall, hearing loop (set your hearing aid to T), large-print missals and bulletins, reserved pews for walkers and wheelchairs. See accessibility.
    • Livestream: a new Daily TV Mass each day; a volunteer can set up your TV or tablet.
    • Forms and finances: the office helps with donation receipts, pre-authorized giving and Mass intentions in person or by phone.
    • Referrals: the St. Vincent de Paul conference and our parish nurse volunteer can point you to home care, meal programs and seniors' services in East York.
    • Funeral pre-planning: the office keeps a simple form for your wishes — readings, hymns, cemetery — on file for your family.

    Give your experience

    Retired parishioners run the pantry mornings, count the collection, garden, drive, phone, visit, sew, bake and — more than anyone — pray. If you have time now that you did not have at forty, the parish has a place for it. See volunteer, or come to Prime Time on Wednesday and ask.

    Is there a cost for anything?

    Prime Time membership is $10 a year; trips are priced at cost with subsidies available. Everything else is free.

    I'm caring for an elderly parent.

    Call the office. Communion at home, a visit from a priest, respite through the friendly-call team, and the bereavement group when the time comes are all there for your family.
