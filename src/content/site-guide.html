Who this page is for

The pastor, the parish secretary and whoever looks after the bulletin. No technical background is assumed. Everything described here can be done from a normal web browser.

1. What you are looking at

This demonstration site shows the complete website a parish receives: more than sixty pages covering Mass times, every sacrament, thirty ministries, an events calendar, weekly bulletins, giving, safe environment policies and a contact office. The photographs are free stock images (see the credits); they are swapped for the parish's own photos before launch. The parish name, address, staff and schedule are placeholders that are replaced with yours.

Every page is written with the same building blocks WordPress uses — paragraphs, headings, images, buttons, columns and cards — so nothing has to be rebuilt when the site moves to WordPress. What you see here is what you will get.

2. Where the site lives

The parish site is hosted on WordPress.com on the Personal plan — about C$4 a month, billed yearly, in the parish's own name. WordPress.com handles security updates, backups and hosting; there is no server for anyone to maintain. Since April 2026 the Personal plan includes everything this design needs:

  • A custom domain (for example stcolumbatoronto.ca), free for the first year, with no advertising on the site.
  • Global Styles, custom fonts and custom CSS — so the colours, typefaces and layouts you see here are reproduced exactly.
  • The Form block (contact, registration, prayer requests), the YouTube block (livestream) and the Google Calendar block (events).
  • 6 GB of storage — enough for years of bulletins and photos.
  • Access to WordPress plugins should the parish ever want more, though this site needs none.

Plan details are published at wordpress.com/pricing and change from time to time; we confirm them with you before the parish signs up.

3. Moving the site onto WordPress.com

This is done once, by us, usually within a week of the parish's go-ahead. You do not need to do anything except approve the content and provide photos.

  1. Create the WordPress.com site and domain

    Registered to the parish's own email address so the parish, not the developer, owns it.

  2. Apply the design

    A standard block theme is installed and its Styles set to the parish palette and fonts; the small custom stylesheet is added under Additional CSS. The header (menu, search, Give button) and footer are built as template parts, so they appear on every page automatically.

  3. Import the pages and photos

    All pages are imported in one step from an export file generated from this demo, with the photos uploaded to the Media Library. Links between pages keep working.

  4. Connect the live pieces

    The parish's YouTube channel is linked to the livestream page, a parish Google Calendar is connected to the events page, and forms are pointed at the office email address.

  5. Review and launch

    The pastor and secretary read every page, we make corrections, and the domain goes live. We then walk the office through the edits below in a one-hour session.

4. Everyday updates the office makes itself

Sign in at yourdomain.ca/wp-admin. Each task below takes a few minutes.

Change Mass or confession times

Pages → Mass Times → Edit. Click the time you want to change, type the new one, and press Update. The home page and footer show the same times; update them in the same way (Appearance → Editor → Footer for the footer). We keep the list of every place a time appears in the parish's handover notes.

Add or change an event

Open the parish Google Calendar (the office already uses it) and add the event with its time, location and a two-sentence description. The calendar on the Events page updates on its own. For a headline event you also want featured on the home page, edit the Home page and change the text in one of the three event cards.

Post this week's bulletin

Pages → Weekly Bulletin → Edit. Click the top entry in the list, duplicate it, change the date and title, then click the PDF button and choose Replace to upload the new PDF from your computer. Press Update. The previous bulletins stay in the archive below.

Add a news item

Pages → Parish News → Edit. Copy the top story block (three dots → Duplicate), change the date, headline, text and photo. To put it on the home page, edit Home and change the first entry in "Parish news".

Update staff, clergy or ministry contacts

Each person is a card on Clergy & Staff; each ministry has its own page with a contact line near the top. Click the text and retype it. To change a photo, click the image → Replace.

Read form submissions

Every contact, registration, prayer request, Mass intention and volunteer form also sends an email to the office address. The full list is under Feedback in the dashboard, where entries can be searched and exported.

Add a completely new page

Pages → Add New. Use the Patterns panel to insert a ready-made layout (card grid, FAQ, people cards, steps) that matches the rest of the site, then add it to the menu under Appearance → Editor → Navigation.

5. Things that stay consistent

The office looks after

  • Mass times, office hours, holiday notices
  • Events in Google Calendar
  • Weekly bulletin PDFs
  • News, photos, ministry contacts
  • Replying to form submissions

We look after (support plan)

  • Design, menus, header and footer
  • Domain and hosting renewals
  • Accessibility and mobile checks
  • New sections or larger changes
  • A yearly review with the pastor

6. Standards this site meets

  • Mobile, tablet and desktop — every page is tested at phone, tablet and laptop widths; the menu becomes a full-screen list on phones and the calendar becomes a list.
  • Accessibility — skip link, keyboard-operable menus, labelled forms, visible focus, alt text on images, colour contrast checked; see the accessibility statement.
  • Search engines — titles and descriptions on every page, structured data for the church and events, a sitemap, and fast-loading resized images.
  • Archdiocese of Toronto — Safe Environment, volunteer screening, misconduct reporting, accessibility and privacy pages are included and linked from every footer.
  • Privacy — forms collect only what the office needs; no advertising trackers.
