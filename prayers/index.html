    Our Faith/Prayers

    Prayers

    The prayers every Catholic carries by heart, how to pray the Rosary, and the Divine Mercy Chaplet — the whole parish prayer book on one page.

    In this section

    • Our Faith
    • Becoming Catholic (OCIA)
    • Beliefs & Teachings

    • Pastoral Plan

    Prayer is simply talking with God and letting God talk with you. It can be done in your own words at the kitchen sink, and it can be done with words the Church has prayed for centuries. The set prayers below are the ones you will hear at Mass, at a bedside, at a graveside and in the pews before the 8:30 Mass when the Rosary is said. Learn them and you will never be at a loss for words when you need them. Click any prayer to open its text.

    Common prayers

    The Sign of the Cross

    In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

    Made with the right hand, touching the forehead, the chest, the left shoulder and the right shoulder. It begins and ends almost every Catholic prayer.

    The Our Father (the Lord's Prayer)

    Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; and forgive us our trespasses as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

    The Hail Mary

    Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

    The Glory Be

    Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

    The Apostles' Creed

    I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth, and in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord, who was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the Virgin Mary, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died and was buried; he descended into hell; on the third day he rose again from the dead; he ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty; from there he will come to judge the living and the dead. I believe in the Holy Spirit, the holy catholic Church, the communion of saints, the forgiveness of sins, the resurrection of the body, and life everlasting. Amen.

    Act of Contrition

    O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended thee, and I detest all my sins because of thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend thee, my God, who art all good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of thy grace, to sin no more and to avoid the near occasions of sin. Amen.

    Said in the confessional after confessing your sins. Any sincere expression of sorrow in your own words is also acceptable. See Reconciliation.

    Hail, Holy Queen (Salve Regina)

    Hail, holy Queen, Mother of mercy, our life, our sweetness and our hope. To thee do we cry, poor banished children of Eve; to thee do we send up our sighs, mourning and weeping in this valley of tears. Turn, then, most gracious advocate, thine eyes of mercy toward us, and after this our exile show unto us the blessed fruit of thy womb, Jesus. O clement, O loving, O sweet Virgin Mary. Pray for us, O holy Mother of God, that we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ. Amen.

    Prayer to St. Michael the Archangel

    St. Michael the Archangel, defend us in battle. Be our protection against the wickedness and snares of the devil. May God rebuke him, we humbly pray; and do thou, O Prince of the heavenly host, by the power of God, cast into hell Satan and all the evil spirits who prowl about the world seeking the ruin of souls. Amen.

    Grace before meals

    Bless us, O Lord, and these thy gifts, which we are about to receive from thy bounty, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

    After meals: We give thee thanks, almighty God, for all thy benefits, who livest and reignest for ever and ever. Amen.

    Parish prayer to St. Columba

    St. Columba, who left the shore of your own country to carry the light of Christ to a people not your own, pray for this parish of travellers and their children. Teach us to make a home for the stranger, to keep the faith in a new land, and to sail wherever the Lord sends us without fear. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

    Written for the parish's 70th anniversary and prayed at the end of the Prayer of the Faithful on our patronal feast, June 9.

    How to pray the Rosary

    The Rosary is a meditation on twenty scenes, called mysteries, from the lives of Jesus and Mary, prayed on a string of beads. It takes about twenty minutes and is prayed in the church every weekday after the 8:30 AM Mass; anyone may join, and there are spare rosaries in the basket by the Mary altar. Beginners often find it easiest to pray with the group a few times before trying alone.

    1. Begin

      Holding the crucifix, make the Sign of the Cross and pray the Apostles' Creed. On the first bead pray an Our Father; on the next three, a Hail Mary each (for faith, hope and love); then a Glory Be.

    2. Announce the first mystery

      Say the name of the mystery (for example, "the first Joyful Mystery, the Annunciation") and picture the scene for a moment. Pray an Our Father on the large bead.

    3. Pray the decade

      Pray a Hail Mary on each of the ten small beads while keeping the mystery in mind, then a Glory Be. Many add the Fátima prayer: "O my Jesus, forgive us our sins, save us from the fires of hell, and lead all souls to heaven, especially those most in need of thy mercy."

    4. Repeat for five mysteries

      Announce the next mystery, Our Father, ten Hail Marys, Glory Be, and so on around the beads.

    5. Finish

      Pray the Hail, Holy Queen, then the Sign of the Cross. Some add a prayer for the Pope's intentions or for a particular need.

    The mysteries by day of the week

    During Advent and Christmas the Joyful Mysteries are often prayed on Sundays, and during Lent the Sorrowful.

    The Divine Mercy Chaplet

    The Chaplet is prayed on ordinary rosary beads and takes about seven minutes. It was given to St. Faustina Kowalska in the 1930s and is prayed in our parish at 3:00 PM on Fridays in Lent and on Divine Mercy Sunday, the Sunday after Easter. Its shape:

    • Begin with the Sign of the Cross, an Our Father, a Hail Mary and the Apostles' Creed.
    • On each large bead: "Eternal Father, I offer you the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world."
    • On each of the ten small beads: "For the sake of his sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world."
    • After five decades, three times: "Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us and on the whole world."
